RingMeFor

Help and support

A real alarm for the messages you can't miss, even on Silent or in a Focus.

For iPhone, with iOS 27 or later.

Set up · Stopping an alarm · If it didn't ring · Pro · Questions · Contact

Good to know

  • RingMeFor needs iOS 27 or later. iOS doesn't let apps read other apps' notifications, so a Shortcuts automation, the RingMeFor shortcut, passes them to RingMeFor. You set it up once.
  • Like any automation, it depends on iOS delivering the notification and running the shortcut, and iOS can skip a run, especially when several notifications arrive within seconds. Use RingMeFor as a backup for the messages you care about, not as an emergency, medical or safety alarm. If an app offers Critical Alerts, keep them on.
  • After your iPhone restarts, nothing rings until you unlock it once.
  • Alarms from a RingMeFor that's hidden or requires Face ID never ring.

Set up

  1. Open RingMeFor and follow the setup: it explains the alarms, iOS asks whether RingMeFor may set alarms, and you pick the apps that should ring.
  2. Tap Connect next to an app, then Add the RingMeFor shortcut, then Add Shortcut. One shortcut covers all your apps. It comes with common chat and email apps; most other apps aren't in it yet, and the Connect screen tells you whether yours is.
  3. Shortcuts then shows All Shortcuts. Tap ‹ at the top left to go back to Library, tap Automation, and turn on the switch next to “When I get a notification from…”. Shared shortcuts arrive switched off.
  4. Go back to RingMeFor and tap Open the RingMeFor shortcut. Tap ⓘ at the bottom, then Privacy, and turn on Allow Running When Locked, so it works while your iPhone is locked.
  5. While you're in the shortcut, look at the apps after “When I receive a notification from”. If your app isn't one of them, tap the apps, tap +, pick your app, then tap Done. Only apps on your iPhone can be picked.
  6. Back in RingMeFor, open Settings → Test with the Phone Locked and tap Start the Test. If iOS asks whether RingMeFor may send notifications, allow it: the test uses a notification from RingMeFor itself. Then lock your iPhone. The first time, go to the Home Screen instead, so you can answer if Shortcuts asks whether RingMeFor can run: tap Always Allow, then run the test again with your iPhone locked. A test alarm rings within about 20 seconds; if it doesn't, the screen lists what to check.
  7. Wait for a notification from your app, or ask someone to message you there. When RingMeFor hears from it, the app's row on the Home screen says Connected. A message rings only if it matches the app's rule: see what a ready-made app rings for.

There's no need to run the shortcut yourself with ▶. It runs by itself when a notification arrives. Keep its name, RingMeFor: RingMeFor's buttons open it by that name.

Remove apps you don't use from the RingMeFor shortcut (open it and tap the apps after “When I receive a notification from”). Every notification from a listed app runs the shortcut, and iOS can skip one that arrives while another is being checked.

Adding an app later

On RingMeFor's Home screen, tap Add Apps and pick the app, or type its name under Another app. Then tap the app and its Connect button, and follow the steps. If the app isn't in the RingMeFor shortcut yet, open the shortcut, tap the apps after “When I receive a notification from”, tap +, pick the app, then tap Done.

Free RingMeFor rings for one app, with one rule. You can still add as many apps as you like: the others show Needs Pro to ring, and you can choose which app is the one that rings (see RingMeFor Pro).

Turning it on and off

At the top of the Home screen, tap On, For… (1, 2, 4 or 8 hours) or Off. While it's on for a set time, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen shows how long is left, with a Turn Off button.

You can also ask Siri: “Turn on RingMeFor” (for the time in Settings), “Turn on RingMeFor for 2 hours”, “Keep RingMeFor on”, “Turn off RingMeFor” or “Is RingMeFor on?”.

To add the Control Center controls, open Control Center, tap +, then Add a Control, and pick RingMeFor. There are two buttons: Turn On RingMeFor (for the time in Settings) and Turn Off RingMeFor, which also cancels alarms already set. They're buttons rather than a switch, so they don't light up to show whether RingMeFor is on; the Home screen does.

To skip one app for a while, turn off its switch on the Home screen. Turning RingMeFor or an app off also cancels repeats that are already set.

Stopping an alarm

Slide Stop on the alarm screen, or tap Stop if the alarm shows at the top of your unlocked iPhone. Pressing the side button or a volume button silences the alarm too.

Snooze silences the alarm, and it rings again after the time in Settings → Snooze for (1 to 15 minutes). A countdown shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island meanwhile, with Cancel Snooze if you don't need it. Ring again is different: it's for when you don't answer at all. If you don't tap Stop or Snooze, the alarm rings again, up to 3 more times.

If you put the alarm screen away, open RingMeFor (or tap the alarm in the Dynamic Island): a ringing or snoozed alarm shows at the top of every screen, with Stop and Snooze.

Ring again (RingMeFor Pro): if the rule has Ring again on, the alarm rings up to 3 more times, titled “Still: …”, until you stop it. Stop or Snooze ends the repeats, and History then shows, for example, “Stopped 12:03, repeats cancelled”. Turning RingMeFor off, turning the app off, removing it or deleting the rule also cancels repeats already set.

Pressing the side or volume button, swiping the alarm away on an unlocked iPhone, or stopping it on Apple Watch may not end the repeats. If a “Still:” alarm rings after you silenced one, slide Stop on it.

If it didn't ring

In RingMeFor, tap the app on the Home screen, then Why Didn't It Ring?. It checks everything RingMeFor can see, and lists what to look at. The usual causes:

  • The app isn't in the RingMeFor shortcut. Open the shortcut and look at the apps after “When I receive a notification from”.
  • The automation is switched off: Shortcuts → Library → Automation.
  • Allow Running When Locked is off, so nothing rings while your iPhone is locked.
  • RingMeFor is hidden or requires Face ID. Alarms from an app that's hidden or requires Face ID never ring. Touch and hold the RingMeFor icon: if the menu says Don't Require Face ID, tap it.
  • A Focus, such as Sleep, or Scheduled Summary may be holding back the app's notifications. Allow the app in each Focus you use (Settings → Focus), and keep it out of Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary.
  • The app didn't send a notification. Check Settings → Notifications and the app's own settings: some apps only notify for mentions.
  • RingMeFor is off, the app's switch on RingMeFor's Home screen is off, or the app shows Needs Pro to ring: free RingMeFor rings for one app.
  • No rule matched, or the rule was outside its hours or in its quiet time. On the app's page, Test a Message shows what a message would do. History says why each notification did or didn't ring; to see the ones no rule matched, tap •••, then Show Every Notification.
  • An alarm for that app was still ringing. A new notification from the same app doesn't add a second alarm while one is ringing.
  • iOS didn't run the shortcut. If History has nothing near that time, this is the likely cause: iOS sometimes doesn't run it, especially when several notifications arrive within seconds.
  • Alarms aren't allowed for RingMeFor. The Home screen says so; tap the button there to fix it.
  • Your iPhone restarted and hasn't been unlocked since.
  • Your alarm volume is low. In Settings → Sounds & Haptics, check the Ringtone slider and, under Alarms and Timers, Match Ringtone Volume and its slider.

Then run Settings → Test with the Phone Locked to check the whole chain. To hear an alarm without the shortcut, tap the app, then Ring a Test Alarm; it rings in a few seconds.

RingMeFor Pro

A one-time purchase, with no subscription, that adds every app, unlimited rules, times and days, Ring again, alarm sounds and the Ring Alarm action for your own shortcuts. Free RingMeFor rings for one app, with one rule; your other apps show Needs Pro to ring, and their rules are kept. To make a different app the one that rings, open it in RingMeFor and switch to it; RingMeFor asks you to confirm.

On a new iPhone, or after reinstalling, Pro usually comes back by itself. If it doesn't, tap Settings → Restore Purchases, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with. Pro isn't shared through Family Sharing.

Apple handles payments and refunds: to ask for a refund, go to reportaproblem.apple.com. After a refund, RingMeFor goes back to the free version once your iPhone hears about it from the App Store, and keeps your apps and rules.

Questions

I'm on iOS 26.

RingMeFor needs iOS 27 or later, because the Shortcuts automation it relies on, “When I get a notification from…”, came with iOS 27. To update, open Settings → General → Software Update.

Do I need to keep RingMeFor open?

No. When a notification arrives from one of the RingMeFor shortcut's apps, iOS runs the shortcut, which starts RingMeFor for a moment to check it.

What does a ready-made app ring for?

Each ready-made app comes with a first rule, and most ring only for certain words, so an everyday “hi” doesn't ring. For example, Discord rings for “urgent”, “emergency”, “@everyone”, “@here” and “wake up”; most email apps for “urgent”, “asap” and “emergency”; camera apps for people, the doorbell and smoke, but not motion; and payment apps for money you receive, such as “paid you”. Reminders and Calendar ring for every reminder and event. To change what rings, tap the app, then its rule. Test a Message on the app's page shows what a message would do.

Does it use much battery?

It's made to use little. RingMeFor doesn't run in the background on its own and uses no network. iOS starts it for a moment for each notification from the apps in the RingMeFor shortcut, including apps you haven't added in RingMeFor, so remove apps you don't use from the shortcut. Settings → Battery shows how much it uses on your iPhone.

Does it work in Low Power Mode?

It should: Low Power Mode doesn't turn off notifications or alarms, though some apps check for new messages less often in it, and RingMeFor can only ring once the notification arrives. It hasn't been tested on every iPhone, so turn on Low Power Mode and run Settings → Test with the Phone Locked to check yours.

Does it ring during Sleep or another Focus?

The alarm itself rings through Silent mode and any Focus. It isn't confirmed yet whether iOS runs the RingMeFor shortcut for a notification that your Focus silences. To be safe, allow the apps you rely on in each Focus you use, such as Sleep (Settings → Focus), and keep them out of Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary. That also lets their other notifications through in that Focus. For the nightly check, allow RingMeFor too.

What does Allow Running When Locked do?

It lets the RingMeFor shortcut run while your iPhone is locked, which is when you need the alarm most. Without it, nothing rings while your iPhone is locked. It applies only to this shortcut, which does one thing: pass each notification to RingMeFor.

My iPhone restarted. Do I need to do anything?

Unlock it once. After a restart, iOS keeps RingMeFor's data locked until you first unlock your iPhone, so nothing rings until then. iPhones restart for automatic iOS updates, when the battery runs out, and after about 72 hours without being unlocked. If you rely on RingMeFor overnight, turn off automatic iOS updates (Settings → General → Software Update → Automatic Updates), or unlock your iPhone after an update.

It stopped working after an iOS update.

Unlock your iPhone once, then run Settings → Test with the Phone Locked. If no alarm rings, the screen lists what to check: RingMeFor in the shortcut's apps, the automation switch (Shortcuts → Library → Automation) and Allow Running When Locked. If it still doesn't work, add the shortcut again: tap your app on RingMeFor's Home screen, then Set Up the Connection Again, I don't have the RingMeFor shortcut and Add the RingMeFor shortcut, and add your apps to it if they aren't listed. Then delete the old copy in Shortcuts. To hear about it sooner next time, turn on the nightly check on the same test screen.

Check Notification shows as unavailable in the shortcut.

On some iPhones with iOS 27, apps' actions have gone missing from Shortcuts for a while. Open RingMeFor once, then restart your iPhone. If it still shows, try again the next day, and email me if it doesn't clear.

Two messages arrived, but only one alarm rang.

While an alarm for an app is ringing, more notifications from that app don't add a second alarm; once you stop it, the next one rings. A rule's quiet time after an alarm also holds it back for a while. And iOS sometimes doesn't run the shortcut, especially when several notifications arrive within seconds. History shows which notifications reached RingMeFor and what it did with each (tap •••, then Show Every Notification).

It rang for the wrong message.

History shows which rule rang and the word or person that matched. Open that rule and add a word under Never ring if it mentions, turn on Match whole words only, or switch it from Every notification to Only certain words or people. Test a Message on the app's page shows what a message would do.

Why do I get so many alarms?

Every matching notification rings, so a rule set to Every notification rings for every message from that app. Open the rule and choose Only certain words or people, or set Quiet time after an alarm. For a break, tap Off on the Home screen, or turn off that app's switch.

A person rule didn't ring.

A person counts when their name is the notification's sender or title, or starts a line of the message, like “Alice: are you up?” in a group chat. A message that only mentions the name doesn't count. Capital letters and accents don't matter. Test a Message on the app's page shows what a message would do.

Does the alarm show my messages on the Lock Screen?

Not unless you turn that on. For chat and email apps, the alarm shows the app and who the message is from, such as “Discord · Alice”. For reminders, calendar, alerts, trading, payments and cameras, it shows the app and the rule's name, such as “Reminders · Due reminders”. Turn on Settings → Show the message to add the first line: “Discord · Alice: Are you up?”. Alarms show on the Lock Screen, in StandBy and on a paired Apple Watch even if you hide notification previews, so leave Show the message off if others can see your iPhone.

Does it work with Apple Watch or CarPlay?

If your iPhone is paired with an Apple Watch, the alarm also shows on the watch, with Stop and Snooze. It isn't confirmed yet whether Stop on the watch also ends Ring again's repeats; if a “Still:” alarm rings afterwards, stop it on your iPhone. RingMeFor has no CarPlay app of its own, and how its alarms show in CarPlay hasn't been tested.

Why does RingMeFor send me a notification every night?

That's the nightly check, if you turned it on. At 9:00 PM, or the time you chose, RingMeFor sends itself a notification without a sound, and the RingMeFor shortcut passes it back. If it doesn't come back within 5 minutes, you get a warning, so you find out before you rely on it. To stop it, turn the nightly check off on the Test with the Phone Locked screen. Don't turn off RingMeFor's notifications instead: the check needs them to warn you.

I got a new iPhone, restored a backup or reinstalled RingMeFor.

If you restored a backup, your apps, rules and History come back; otherwise, set RingMeFor up again. Pro comes back with your Apple Account; if it doesn't, tap Settings → Restore Purchases. On a new or restored iPhone, the RingMeFor automation may arrive switched off even though RingMeFor still says Connected. Turn it on in Shortcuts → Library → Automation, check Allow Running When Locked (open the shortcut, tap ⓘ, then Privacy), then run Settings → Test with the Phone Locked. If you add the shortcut again, delete the old copy, or each notification is checked twice.

Can I use it on an iPad, or on more than one iPhone?

RingMeFor is made for iPhone. It may install on an iPad, but it isn't made or tested for one. On a second iPhone, set RingMeFor up there too: apps and rules aren't shared between iPhones. If you use iCloud for Shortcuts, the RingMeFor shortcut may already be there with its automation switched off: turn it on and check Allow Running When Locked. Pro works on every iPhone signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with.

I added the shortcut twice.

Keep one: with two copies, each notification is checked twice. In Shortcuts, touch and hold the copy you don't need, then tap Delete, and make sure the one you keep is named RingMeFor.

Nothing happens when I tap Add the RingMeFor shortcut or Open the RingMeFor shortcut.

Both open Apple's Shortcuts app, so check that it's installed; if you deleted it, get Shortcuts again from the App Store. Open the RingMeFor shortcut finds the shortcut by its name: if you renamed it, open Shortcuts, tap Library and open it from there, or rename it back to RingMeFor.

An app I didn't add appeared in RingMeFor.

The RingMeFor shortcut passed a notification from an app RingMeFor doesn't know, so RingMeFor added it by name, with no rules. It doesn't ring, and none of its messages are kept until you add a rule to it or turn it on. This happens when you add an app to the shortcut yourself, or when iOS reports an app under another name, such as another app from the same company or the app's name in your iPhone's language. If you don't want it, open it and tap the Remove button at the bottom of its page: RingMeFor won't add it again.

History shows “Unknown app”.

The shortcut passed a notification without saying which app it came from, or with something that looks like a message instead of an app's name. History shows the first 40 characters of what arrived as the app's name, so you can fix the shortcut; the message isn't kept. In a shortcut you made yourself, set the Check Notification action's App to the notification's App; the ready-made RingMeFor shortcut already does this.

History shows a notification I never got.

RingMeFor can't tell where a notification really came from: any shortcut in your Shortcuts library can pass it any text as if it were a notification. Check Shortcuts → Library for shortcuts you don't recognize, and only add shortcuts you trust.

Does RingMeFor read my messages?

It sees only the notifications the RingMeFor shortcut passes it, from the apps listed in the shortcut, and only on your iPhone. It checks the ones from apps you added in RingMeFor against your rules, and History keeps up to 140 characters of each. Notifications from ready-made apps you haven't added are ignored and not kept, and an app RingMeFor added by itself keeps none of its messages until you give it a rule or turn it on. To keep an app's notifications from reaching RingMeFor at all, remove it from the shortcut's apps. Nothing is sent to the developer or anyone else: see the privacy policy.

Why does the free version ring for only one app?

One app with one rule, with no time limit, is enough to check that the whole chain rings on your iPhone before you pay. RingMeFor Pro, a one-time purchase, rings for all your apps and adds unlimited rules, times and days, Ring again, alarm sounds and the Ring Alarm action. Either way you can add as many apps as you like; in the free version the others show Needs Pro to ring.

Can it ring for calls, texts or iMessages?

For calls and messages from people who matter, iOS has Emergency Bypass, which lets them sound even in Silent mode or a Focus: in Contacts, open the person, tap Edit, then Ringtone or Text Tone, and turn on Emergency Bypass. With RingMeFor Pro, you can also add RingMeFor's Ring Alarm action to a Shortcuts automation of your own, such as one that runs when you get a message from someone.

Does it need an internet connection? What about abroad or with a VPN?

RingMeFor itself doesn't use the internet, so roaming or a VPN doesn't change what it does. The apps you watch need a connection to get their notifications, and buying or restoring Pro needs the App Store. Rules with times follow your iPhone's clock, so they move with your time zone.

How do I pause it, for example for the weekend?

Tap Off on the Home screen, or say “Turn off RingMeFor”, and turn it back on when you're ready. To pause one app, turn off its switch on the Home screen. With RingMeFor Pro, a rule can ring only at certain times and on certain days, such as weekdays.

Siri says it can't find RingMeFor.

Say the name as three words, “Ring Me For”: for example, “Turn on Ring Me For”. If Siri still doesn't find it, open RingMeFor once, then try again. The buttons on RingMeFor's Home screen always work.

Is RingMeFor in my language?

Its screens are in English. Your rules work in any language: capital letters and accents don't matter, and words in Chinese, Japanese and Thai match inside sentences.

Accessibility

RingMeFor's alarms are iOS alarms, with Stop and Snooze, and they also show on a paired Apple Watch. Its screens follow Dark Mode. If something in RingMeFor is hard to use with VoiceOver, larger text or another accessibility setting, please email me: it helps me fix it.

Removing RingMeFor

Delete the RingMeFor shortcut too, or its automation keeps running for every notification from its apps, with nothing to pass them to: in Shortcuts, touch and hold RingMeFor, then tap Delete. Deleting RingMeFor deletes your apps, rules and History from your iPhone; your own backups may keep a copy until they're replaced. To clear only History, tap History, then •••, then Clear History.
